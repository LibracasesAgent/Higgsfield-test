# Halbert's *349 Great Headlines / 152 Attention Grabber Words* (owner upload, 2026-11-05)

Source: Gary C. Halbert, *Killer Advertising Secrets: 349 Great Headlines, 152 Attention Grabber Words, Sample Ads, Sample Letters*
(scanned PDF, 41 pages; uploaded by the owner to the session, not in the repo). Read by OCR. [V] = on the page.

What we took (into `pipeline/data/rulebook.json hooks.formulas` and `docs/RULEBOOK.md` section 3):
- The **shapes** that repeat across the 349 headlines [V]: "How To ...", the yes-question ("Are You Ever Tongue-Tied At A Party?",
  "Do You Have These Symptoms ...?"), a number of things ("7 Ways To ...", "The 5 Most Costly Mistakes ... How Many Are You Making?"),
  the warning ("Using A Lawyer May Be Dangerous To Your Wealth"), the contrast ("Don't Try This With Any Other Copier"), the command
  ("Throw Away Your Oars!"), the scene ("Suppose This Happened On Your Wedding Day!"), news ("At Last", "New"), "The Truth About ...",
  "What Everybody Ought To Know About ...", "Imagine ...", "If You Can X - You Can Y", "For The Woman Who ...", "Who Else ...", the
  hidden thing ("Profits That Lie Hidden In Your Farm"), the price as news ("Take Any 3 Of These ... For Only $8.95").
- The principle behind them [I]: the headline flags one reader with her own interest, in concrete words, and promises a specific thing.
- About 30 of the 152 attention words that fit a handbag brand (at last, test drive, buyer's guide, show me, unlock, timely, savvy ...).

What we did NOT take [I]: most of the book's headlines sell health, weight loss, income, gambling, government money or sex
("NEW PILL GIVES ALMOST COMPLETE RELIEF FROM ARTHRITIS PAIN!", "DESPERATE WOMAN LOSES 277 POUNDS"), with "miracle", "secret",
"guaranteed", "amazing" and invented testimonials. Those break Meta's policies and our compliance rules (PLAYBOOK section 9); the
rulebook lists them as words to avoid. The sample ads and letters (pages 20-41: long-copy mail order) do not transfer to 3-second video
hooks beyond one idea: open with something that makes one reader feel addressed.
