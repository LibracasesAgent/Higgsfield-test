## 1. Who buys practical "luxury-look" / anti-theft handbags, and what they say

**Who**
- Travelon (category leader): "We primarily sell our bags to women who are 30-70 years old"; most are worried about safety and buy for trips outside the US (2015 quote). [V] https://www.foxnews.com/travel/why-this-bag-is-selling-out-on-qvc.amp
- Circana (2023): over 60% of women 35+ always carry a handbag outside work or school, against 39% of women 18-34; handbag units +7% among older women, -2% among 18-34; small crossbodies are the style carried most. [V] https://www.giftsanddec.com/analytics/fewer-young-women-are-carrying-handbags-these-days-what-does-that-mean-for-sales-totals
- Pew (Jan-Jun 2026): Facebook used by 71% of US adults 50-64 and 59% of those 65+; 77% of women against 63% of men. Instagram reaches only 41% of 50-64. [V] https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/
- Pew 2025: 54% of adults 50-64 visit Facebook daily. [V] https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/
- AARP 2026: 64% of adults 50+ expect to travel in 2026; "89% shop for bargains"; they took 4.2 trips in 2025. [V] https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2026-03-10-aarp-travel-trends-2026/
- Sixty and Me (an audience of women 60+) writes that older women are "seen as easy targets", and its readers want to stop constantly checking their bag, plus a bag that survives cobblestones and transit and still works at dinner. An ad can't say that about the viewer (Meta personal-attributes rule). [V]/[I] https://sixtyandme.com/?p=74022

**Why they buy** (ranked by how often the reviews below say it) [I]
- 1) Safety in crowds and on trips, 2) organisation and finding things fast, 3) not looking like a tourist / looking put-together, 4) value ("looks expensive"), 5) hands free. Comfort and pain ("Perfect for my arthritis" on a competitor page) is a health claim: we never use it. [I] https://peaksta.com/pages/anti-theft-travel-tips-europe

**Recurring delights**
- Pockets and compartments, finding things without digging, compliments, soft feel, light weight, zips that lock in restaurants. [V] https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9840244/page/3 , https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9721613

## 2. Advertising to women 45-65 on Facebook (2025-2026)

- **The face steers delivery.** In a peer-reviewed study with neutral targeting, "the age of the person pictured correlates positively with the age of the users to whom it is shown." Images of middle-aged and elderly people reached 78.2% and 80.5% older users, against 70.5-75.6% for younger subjects. Older women in the image: 58% of the audience was women. Young women in the image: shown disproportionately to men 55+. [V] https://facebook-targeting.ccs.neu.edu/measurement/papers/kaplan2022measurement.pdf
- So for this audience, casting works as targeting. A presenter who reads 50-60 pulls delivery toward women 45-65; one who reads 30 pulls it elsewhere. [I]
- AARP Mirror/Mirror (Nov 2022, n=7,368): 68% of women 50+ "rarely" or "never" see themselves in media and ads. About three quarters of women are more likely to buy from advertisers that represent their age. Only 20% of women 50+ use filters. [V] https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/style-trends/info-2023/women-beauty-standards/
- Over-50s survey (UK, self-selected): 85% say ads for older people rely on stereotypes; 55% hate words like "older", "silver", "mature" and "senior"; 28% dislike casting that is too young. [S] https://www.campaignindia.in/article/one-wrong-word-and-you-lose-them-the-perils-of-marketing-to-mature-consumers/4yav0t28qmx9n1xne2mg2yaymd
- **Calm beats hype for older viewers.** In the study, 47 adults aged 60-85 remembered brands better in calm commercials and liked them more. The authors write that "a large number of camera changes, sound effects, loud upbeat music or overly excited voices do not seem to be beneficial." Younger adults (18-25) showed the opposite. [V] https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/2693612/168882_502304.pdf
- FDA/RTI (n=1,075): age lowered comprehension; 41.6% of viewers 75+ got the ad's main point, against 60.7% of 18-25. Faster, more complex sentences lowered recall at every age. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7342495
- For our 185-215 wpm format: keep the pace (it's the owner's measured format) but make each sentence carry one simple idea. Keep the voice warm rather than excited, the music soft, no whooshes or sound effects. Show every number on screen as well as saying it. The presenter projects (a woman sitting and talking) are the natural calm variant. [I]
- System1/ITV Wise Up (2022, 56 ads): an average of 3.76 stars with the general public against 2.96 with older audiences; only 5 of 56 ads scored higher with older viewers, who criticised generic content. ITV's advice: "stories featuring older characters who are authentic, valued and part of a greater whole". [V] https://uk.themedialeader.com/older-people-ignored-in-tv-advertising/
- Facebook still performs for this audience: "performance from 40+ women continues to be very strong and a consistent segment" (agency, 2021). Laura Geller put ~80% of its media budget on Facebook and Instagram. [V] https://digiday.com/marketing/trying-to-be-where-she-is-why-beauty-brand-targeting-women-over-40-is-still-reliant-on-facebook
- On-screen text: high-contrast sans-serif supers read best for older viewers; WCAG requires 4.5:1 contrast, and senior-design guides suggest 7:1. [S] https://www.digitalthriveai.com/en-us/resources/web-design/guide-designing-older-adults/
- Tone: speak at eye level, neither up nor down; humour is "difficult to get right"; informational, editorial-style ads work well. [S] https://www.campaignindia.in/article/one-wrong-word-and-you-lose-them-the-perils-of-marketing-to-mature-consumers/4yav0t28qmx9n1xne2mg2yaymd
- A 2026 benchmark says UGC still beats studio creative on Facebook, but only by 15-25%, and older Facebook audiences "respond better to production quality". [S] https://www.finsi.ai/blog/ugc-ads-performance-benchmarks/
- Not found: any controlled Meta test of creator age, text size or pace for women 45-65. [U]

## 3. Gift buyers in November and December

- Numerator, Cyber Weekend 2025 (9,199 verified buyers, 27 Nov - 1 Dec): 92% bought a gift; 63% said more than half or all of their purchases were gifts; 28% said all of them were; 56% shopped mostly online. [V] https://www.numerator.com/press/64-of-cyber-weekend-buyers-say-rising-prices-impacted-their-shopping-56-shopped-primarily-or-exclusively-online-numerator-reports/
- Deloitte 2026 (n=4,000, 26 Aug - 10 Sep): 47% prefer "one meaningful, higher-cost gift" to several small ones; 38% are value seekers who plan to spend 12% more; 77% shop in late November and 78% in early December; 28% expect their budget to run out by the end of October. [V] https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deloitte-holiday-spending-set-to-increase-4-while-shoppers-focus-on-value-302909264.html
- Self-gifting, Deloitte 2026: 18% of boomers against 49% of Gen Z; women plan to spend $245 on themselves. Clothing and accessories are the top self-gift category (64%), a figure seen only in a secondary summary. [V] same URL; [S] https://ppc.land/self-gifting/
- Shopify-Gallup (2023): 55% of shoppers 50+ planned to buy for themselves or their household during BFCM (71% of those under 50). [V] https://www.audacy.com/kdwn/news/64-of-americans-buy-for-themselves-on-black-friday
- Pinterest: self-gift wishlists "build momentum from October and peak in November", ahead of the December rush of gifts for others. [S] https://ppc.land/self-gifting/
- Meta/YouGov (Dec 2024, 18,727 shoppers, 14 countries; Meta-commissioned): 48% want gift ideas from brands on Reels; 50% discovered brands on Meta during the festive season. [S] https://ppc.land/meta-releases-2025-festive-season-guide-with-ai-campaign-tools/
- Circana, Mother's Day 2025: one third of moms say their families never or rarely "get it right"; flowers and practical items are what they mostly receive. [V] https://www.giftsanddec.com/industry-news/mothers-day-2025-shopping-trends-circana/
- Real gift language: "Got this for my wife for our anniversary."; "Received as a gift!" [V] https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9223406 , https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9721613
- Libra's own: "Got this bag for my daughter for Christmas and she adores it." (Felicia S.); "Already bought for friends." (Carol R.), both client-approved. [V] pipeline/data/reviews.json
- Gift guides frame an anti-theft bag as a way "to show that you care about their safety". [S] https://www.stylecraze.com/articles/best-anti-theft-purses
- "Men shop last minute" data conflicts: 40% of Texas men wait until Christmas Eve (CouponBirds 2022) against 8.9% shopping late (Numerator 2019). Don't build on it. [U] https://www.sacurrent.com/news/40-of-texas-men-admit-they-wait-until-christmas-eve-to-buy-their-partners-gifts-30578951/
- No handbag "gift for her" ad with published results was found. [U]
- Timing: November = Black Friday sale plus self-gifting ("for you"); early December = gift-for-her (mum, daughter, sister, friend) using the site's real shipping cut-off; stop "in time for Christmas" lines after that date. [I]

## 4. Bundles, AOV and compliant price anchoring

- 243 DTC ad/landing-page teardowns (Feb-Aug 2026): only 49 of 243 ads put an offer in the frame. Bundle/kit ads (6 of 10 broke the hand-off) and dollar savings (6 of 11) did best, attributed to "naming an exact product at an exact price". Free gifts did worst (10 of 11). A deeper % didn't help: 11 of the 25 percentage ads printed 40%+. [V] https://taylorsicard.com/blog/dtc-ad-promotions-new-customers
- Agency claim: bundle ads raise AOV 23-38% when run next to single-product ads (own clients, no method). It advises showing the struck-through individual prices beside the bundle price, as in "Save $19.50 (25% off)". [S] https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/dtc-bundle-ads-strategy/
- Vendor roundups give bundle AOV lifts of 15-55%, attributed to McKinsey and others without links. [S] https://www.ringly.io/blog/ecommerce-aov-statistics-2026
- Rule of 100 (Berger): under $100 a percentage discount feels bigger; over $100 a dollar amount does. [S] https://jonahberger.com/fuzzy-math-what-makes-something-seem-like-a-good-deal/
- Decoy/compromise pricing: in Ariely's Economist test the bundle took 84% with a decoy and 32% without it; critics say it rarely replicates outside the lab. [S] https://atticusli.com/replication-crisis/decoy-effect-asymmetric-dominance/
- For Libra: the Hobo alone ($54.95) beside the 3-Piece Set ($69.95) is a natural compromise pair. Lead the set with its % (under $100) and add the dollar step: "$15 more and you get the crossbody and the pouch too". Check both prices live on the site before each batch. [I]
- "One for you, one for her" is real buyer behaviour: "Purchased one for my daughter and one for myself for a recent trip to Europe."; "My daughter and I each got a baggalini for our next trip." [V] https://zappos.com/product/review/9034214 , https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9223406
- FTC 16 CFR 233.1: a former price has to be one the item was actually offered at "for a reasonably substantial period"; a fictitious "was" price makes the bargain false. [V] https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2010-title16-vol1/pdf/CFR-2010-title16-vol1-sec233-1.pdf
- California B&P 17501: no former price unless it "was the prevailing market price ... within three months" before the ad, or the date it prevailed is stated. [V] https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17501
- FTC "Free" guide 16 CFR 251.1: the regular price is the 30-day price; "no more than three such offers ... in any 12-month period", 30 days apart, and no single product carries a Free offer more than six months a year; conditions go "clearly and conspicuously at the outset". [V] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/251.1
- A pouch wallet that is "free" all year sits outside that guide. Raise it with the client. [I]
- What not to copy (Peaksta advertorial, a competing anti-theft crossbody): a timer frozen at "12:00:00", "Only 23 BOGO bundles left", "2,906 bags sold" in 7 days next to "70,000+" sold, a "Sarah from Texas" pop-up, a "Tariff alert" with no date. [V] https://peaksta.com/pages/anti-theft-travel-tips-europe

## 5. Travel safety: true, usable, not sensational

- QuoteZone/Euronews (May 2024): pickpocket mentions in reviews of each country's top five attractions were Italy 478 per million British visitors, France 251, Spain 111, Germany 111 and Netherlands 100. The worst spots were the Trevi Fountain, the Eiffel Tower and Las Ramblas. These are review mentions, not crime data. [V] https://www.euronews.com/travel/2024/05/02/remain-vigilant-europes-most-heavily-pickpocketed-tourist-spots-revealed
- Tripadvisor counts (money.co.uk): Las Ramblas 3,271 reviews mention pickpockets, the Eiffel Tower 2,569, the Louvre 598, Sagrada Familia 407. [S] https://www.nzherald.co.nz/travel/places-you-are-most-likely-to-be-pickpocketed-according-to-tripadvisor/EVZW5RINVPHA6LR3KZXNIARMLQ/
- Barcelona's "100,944 pickpocketing incidents in 2023" is repeated everywhere, but no primary source was found. One report says thefts fell 6.3% in 2024, a decade low. Use neither. [U] https://euroweeklynews.com/2025/05/29/by-day-locals-by-night-civic-guards-barcelonas-new-crimefighters/
- UK government advice, France: "Pickpockets can work in gangs; one distracts you while the other one goes into your bag."; "carry your bag across your body rather than on your shoulder"; "use the inside compartments in bags where possible"; "keep your belongings close to you in restaurants and bars". [V] https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/france/safety-and-security
- UK government advice, Italy: petty crime is higher, "particularly bag-snatching and pickpocketing, in city centres and at major tourist attractions"; take care "in crowded areas and in city centres, including main train and metro stations"; don't carry all your valuables in one handbag. [V] https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/italy/safety-and-security
- Rick Steves: "nearly all crimes suffered by tourists are nonviolent and avoidable"; "There probably aren't more thieves in Europe than in the US."; "Don't travel fearfully — travel carefully." [V] https://www.ricksteves.com/travel-tips/theft-scams/outsmarting-pickpockets
- Meta: "Ads must not contain shocking, sensational or excessively violent content"; the policy page says nothing about theft. The risk lies in the imagery (a snatch, a blade on a strap) and in alarmist wording, not in the topic. [V] https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/sensational-content
- How the security brands phrase it: Pacsafe says "TRAVEL FEARLESSLY" and "Slimline to keep valuables close to your body" and never describes a crime. Travelon says "What worry-free travel looks like" and "5 POINT ANTI-THEFT SECURITY SYSTEM". [V] https://pacsafe.com/ , https://www.travelonbags.com/
- Honesty sells to this buyer. One QVC customer knew her bag "wouldn't deter the most determined of thieves" and wanted to stop opportunists. Say "harder to get into" and "stays against your body", never "theft-proof". [V]/[I] https://www.foxnews.com/travel/why-this-bag-is-selling-out-on-qvc.amp
- Usable frame: quote the advice, not the crime ("Travel advice says: across your body, inside pockets, close to you at the table. This bag is built that way."). Never call a named city dangerous. [I]

## Top 10 takeaways for Libra

1. Cast the buyer: on screen, women who look 45-65. Meta delivers to people like the face in the ad, and 68% of women 50+ rarely see themselves in ads. Never write an age in the copy; show it. [V]/[I]
2. The category's big complaint is that anti-theft bags look like tourist gear and weigh a lot. Libra's opening line: it looks like a real handbag and keeps your things closed and against your body. [I]
3. Say "vegan leather", every time. "Luxury leather" on PU is already producing "not leather" 1-star reviews (Oct 2026), and it's a deception risk under Meta and FTC rules. Check the PLAYBOOK's approved claims for this. [V]/[I]
4. Frame safety with official advice (across your body, inside pockets, close to you at the table), not with crime figures or fear. Never show a theft or a blade; never say "theft-proof"; never call a named city dangerous. [V]/[I]
5. Calm wins with older viewers: a warm, unhurried voice, soft music, no sound effects or hype. Keep the 2 s cutting, but give each sentence one simple idea and put every number on screen in large, high-contrast type. [V]/[I]
6. November-December is gift season (92% of Cyber Weekend buyers bought a gift). Run a project built on real gifting: for a daughter, a mum or a friend, with Libra's own approved reviews ("for my daughter for Christmas", "Already bought for friends"). [V]
7. Treat November as a "for you" window as well: 55% of buyers 50+ shop for themselves in BFCM, and self-gift wishlists peak in November. "You've earned a good bag" works without any first-person claim. [V]/[I]
8. Sell the set as "one for you, one for her" (real behaviour: "one for my daughter and one for myself"). Use the exact product at the exact price: "60% off" (under $100 a % reads bigger) plus "$15 more than the Hobo alone". Re-check the prices live. [V]/[I]
9. Keep price claims defensible: the "was" price must have been real for 3 months (California) or a substantial period (FTC). A pouch wallet that is "free" all year breaks the FTC "Free" guide's limits; take it to the client. No frozen timers, no "only 23 left" (the Peaksta pattern). [V]/[I]
10. Earn trust explicitly: don't cite Trustpilot (its rating is suspended for fake reviews). Use only named verbatim reviews, and make sure the landing page answers the real objections: returns going to Australia, delivery time, email replies. [V]/[I]
