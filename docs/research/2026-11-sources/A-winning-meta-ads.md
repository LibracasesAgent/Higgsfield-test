# What the best Meta ads have in common (DTC, 2025-2026): research A, 2026-11-03

Tags: [V] read on the page itself (numbers or claims); [S] vendor/agency claim, method not shown; [I] my inference; [U] could not open or verify the primary. Builds on docs/research/2026-10-black-friday-and-bag-ads.md (BFCM looks, competitor teardowns, bag hooks, Meta policy): none of that is repeated here. Caveat for the whole file: almost no source measures ROAS; most use spend share or survival (how long an ad keeps running) as the proxy for a winner.

## 1. Anatomy of top-1% Meta video ads

**How rare winners are, and how many ads it takes**
- Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026: 578,750 creatives, 6,015 accounts, $1.29B, launched 1 Sep 2025 - 1 Jan 2026 (the BFCM window). A winner is an ad that spends at least 10x the account median and at least $500 (about the 92nd percentile). Winners are 3.7% (micro) to 8.2% (enterprise) of ads and take ~55% of spend (64% in enterprise accounts); 50-53% of ads are turned off before 28 days. Motion's advice: "Don't expect more than 1 in 10-13 creatives to be winners." https://content.motionapp.com/hubfs/Benchmarks-Report-2026.pdf [V]
- Volume, not judgement, separates accounts. Median creatives per week: micro 2.8, small ($10-50K/mo) 4.1, medium 6.7, large 11.2, enterprise 18.9. The top 25% of accounts ship 4.8 / 8.1 / 16.0 / 31.1 / 54.6 a week. Average hit rate rises from 4.0% to 8.8% by tier. Same PDF [V]
- Motion's talk on the report: 20 ads give about 1-1.6 winners, 50 ads about 2.5-4. https://runneth.com/library/talk/meta-ads-in-2026-how-many-creatives-do-you-actually-need-to-launch/ [V]
- Do not judge too early. At under $100 spend, 45% of eventual winning creator ads still looked like losers; at about $1,000 spend, 26% still did (Agentio, 65,000 partnership ads). https://martechvibe.com/article/creator-ads-deliver-19-higher-ctr-on-meta-partnership-ads [S]

**Formats that win (Motion, all verticals; hit rate = winners / ads)**
- By hit rate: unboxing 9.83%, offer-first banner 8.68%, behind the scenes 8.64%, founder 8.57%, POV 8.28%, demo 8.11%, grid swap 7.98%, influencer endorsement 7.71%. Lower down: testimonial 6.57%, us-vs-them 6.52%, feature-benefit pointout 5.61%, listicle 5.30%, review 4.89%. Report PDF [V]
- Where the money goes: offer-first banners are 21.9% of ads but 29.3% of spend; testimonials take 13.4%, headline ads 13.0% and demos 12.8% of spend. Motion calls offer-first and demo "scale formats". Unboxing, POV, behind-the-scenes and founder ads win often but are "volatile". Report PDF [V]
- By asset type: text-only 11.60%, product image with text 8.75%, lifestyle-product image 7.59%, UGC 7.56%, high-production 6.97%, plain product image 5.39%, carousel 4.45%. Report PDF [V]
- **Fashion & apparel** (hit rate): Post-it note 27.4%, quiz 26.0%, stylized product shot 21.2%, meme 17.3%, ASMR 15.1%, product shot 15.0%, social comments 14.8%, podcast 14.0%, product showcase 14.0%, unconventional text placement 13.4%. Spend-use ratio (share of spend / share of ads): podcast 2.50, unconventional text placement 2.33, billboard 2.00, text message 1.75, sign 1.60, slideshow 1.36, offer-first banner 1.30, demo 1.27. Report PDF, p.14 [V]

**Hooks and first frames**
- Headline and hook tactics by hit rate (BFCM window): newness 11.37%, sale announcement 11.35%, price anchor 10.89%, urgency 9.73%, offer only 9.29%, FOMO 9.20%, confession 8.74%, curiosity 7.77%. Lower: bold claim 7.19%, relatability 6.85%, direct address 6.65%, authority 6.40%. Spend use: confession 1.41, storytelling 0.95, reasons-why 0.92. Motion: "broad lifestyle statements and vague benefit claims ... surface less frequently among winners." Report PDF [V]
- 198 durable winners (60+ days, 169 advertisers, US Ad Library): 55% open with a plain statement of what the product or offer is. Then bold claim / pattern interrupt 14%, question 11%, first-person story 7%, price/offer 7%. https://creativefly.ai/blog/we-tagged-200-winning-ads-2026 [V]
- 10,000 "scaling" ecommerce ads (21+ days, 500k+ reach, growing three weeks running): 82% carry a text overlay in the first second. Openers: pattern interrupt 34%, problem-first 24%, result-first 13%. 64% are UGC-style; 58% are 9:16. The product is shown working between seconds 3 and 8; demos that start after 10 s are rare. Average thumbstop rate 32-38%. https://winninghunter.com/blog/anatomy-of-a-scaling-meta-ad [V] (vendor library, method stated)
- 483 top-spending creatives from 17 US accounts spending $100K+/month (Aug-Sep 2026): text-overlay hooks took 25% of top spend, bold statements 17%, relatable statements 11%, questions 5%. Question hooks still ran at 28% lower cost per result in 5 of 6 accounts. https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/best-performing-meta-ads [V]

**Benchmarks (none published by Meta)**
- Hook rate (3-s views / impressions), cold DTC traffic: Feed about 23%, Reels about 30%. Motion's tiers: under 25% weak, 25-35% solid, over 35% strong. "Elite" is 40%+ (Vaizle). Hold (15-s views / 3-s views): 15-25% for 15-30 s videos, 8-18% for 30-60 s videos. Four incompatible hold formulas are in use. https://www.adsights.ai/resources/guides/video-creative-metrics-benchmarks-2026-guide , https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-top-performing-video-ad-frame-by-frame [S]

**Length**
- Median winning video: 30 s (54% are 30 s or less, 21% run over a minute; 46% of winners are video). https://creativefly.ai/blog/we-tagged-200-winning-ads-2026 [V] Winning Hunter's median is 27 s; 60 s+ ads are "mostly considered purchases above $80". https://winninghunter.com/blog/anatomy-of-a-scaling-meta-ad [V]
- 421 videos, 21 brands, $10.3M (Jul-Oct 2026): cost per result is 0.94-1.03x the brand median in every length bucket (31-45 s 0.94x, over 60 s 0.95x). Hook rate is 27% at 31-60 s but 16% over 60 s. Videos over 60 s are 9% of ads and 8% of spend, and 0.2% of their impressions reach the end. https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/meta-video-ad-length [V]
- Fashion survivors (30+ days): 60% are statics; fashion videos run under 15 s with a visual hook and no voiceover; 20-30 s ads survived worst in every niche (523 ads in all). https://brandsearch.co/blog/winning-meta-ad-patterns-2026 [V]
- CreativeX (1.6M ads): naming the brand in the first 3 s gave +16% completion; 15-60 s gave +10%. Only 3% of creator ads keep inside the safe zones. https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/24/creativex-creator-ads-best-practices/amp/ [V]
- For Libra: no dataset shows 55-80 s beating 25-45 s, and none shows it losing on cost per result. Long ads are cost-neutral at best, with the weakest hook rate. Our $84 order value sits at the "considered purchase" edge where 60 s+ ads still appear. [I]

**Production style and voice**
- Polished ads took 63% of top spend, at 4% below average cost per result. Creator-led ads took 33% of top spend and were the majority in only 4 of 17 accounts (all consumer brands), at 10% above average cost per result. Lo-fi took under 1%. Themes: "how it works" ran at -18% cost per result; "problem & solution" at +9% (colder traffic). https://www.biddyco.com/blog-posts/best-performing-meta-ads [V]

## 2. Meta Andromeda / Advantage+ era

- Andromeda is the retrieval stage: it cuts "tens of millions of ad candidates" to "a few thousand". +6% recall and +8% ads quality on selected segments. Meta built it for the growth in creatives that generative AI brings. https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/ [V]
- Meta, 16 Dec 2025: its system "groups ads that share key visual and thematic attributes". Look-alike ads count as variations of one creative and share learnings. **Iteration** is A/B refinement of one concept, for example identical visuals with different text CTAs. It "should not replace" **diversification**: distinct personas (Gen Z Reels vs a creator ad for new moms), motivations (emotional vs features), look, feel, storyline, message and format. Small tweaks don't count. No ad count is given. https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification [V]
- Meta, 19 Nov 2024: keep at least 20 diversified ads in Advantage+ shopping campaigns, spend 20-30% of budget testing new ads, launch new ads weekly, and build from personas (younger shoppers: deals and value; older: reviews and demos). Partnership ads cut CPA 19% on average. https://www.facebook.com/business/news/three-steps-to-optimize-your-performance-with-creative-diversification [V]
- Meta, Sep 2026 (to Marketing Brew): its advice is "deliberately outcome-based (not model-specific)", and it would not say how its models represent a creative. Agency executives said ads that share a look or an opening hook with the advertiser's other ads are treated as "essentially the same ad". https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-ad-creative-diversification-same-hook-same-ad/ [V]
- Similarity thresholds ("over 60% suppressed", "keep under 40%") and "entity IDs" come from vendors, not Meta. An Ads Manager "Creative diversity" column (Low / Medium / High per ad set) was reported in Aug 2026. https://www.recharm.com/blog/what-is-andromeda-and-creative-similarity , https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/metas-new-creative-diversity-score-is-live-in-ads-manager-what-ecommerce-brands-must-act-on-now [U]
- How winners get iterated (Motion bootcamp, Jun 2026: Ready Set, Happy Mammoth, Caraway). Hook-only or opening-visual swaps "are no longer enough" after Andromeda. Changes that count: new talent or persona, new footage, new pain point, a new body, a new format. Other tactics: "mashups" (a high-thumbstop hook on a high-CVR body from another winner) and 3-5 new batches per winner. Caraway scaled one "reasons not to buy" negative-hook concept to 10+ variants, and its program was ~40 new UGC ads a month (60+ in BFCM) at a 2.14 average UGC ROAS. https://runneth.com/library/talk/scaling-ugc-ads-post-andromeda-one-winner-many-formats/ [V]
- A practitioner who has spent $100M+ (Jan 2026): run 8-15 conceptually different ads; about 1 new concept per $10K monthly spend; judge after 5-7 days or 10-12 purchases per concept; refresh every 7-14 days at scale. https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the [S]
- Fatigue:
  - Most Sep-Jan ads were off before 28 days (Motion PDF [V]).
  - Ecommerce Q1 2026 (31,592 creatives, 133 brands): median lifespan 22 days; 35% survive 30 days, 15% survive 60 days, none survive 90. https://benly.ai/benchmarks/q1-2026/e-commerce-retail [V]
  - Winning creator ads last 36 days on average, then CPA rises 1.8x; keeping 10 winners live takes about 40 new creator ads a month. Martechvibe/Agentio URL above [S]
- Advantage+ creative enhancements:
  - Meta: +8% conversions per dollar (over 1M ad sets); 9:16 video with audio adds +7%; use at least 3 asset types. https://ppc.land/meta-releases-2025-festive-season-guide-with-ai-campaign-tools/ [V]
  - Practitioners turn off text rewriting, generated backgrounds and music where claims or brand matter; one agency got +26% ROAS by turning text improvements off. https://metalla.digital/meta-advantage-plus-creative-enhancements/ [S]

## 3. Comparison ("us vs them", "versus") ads

- Why they work: a meta-analysis (Grewal et al., Journal of Marketing 1997) found comparative ads beat non-comparative ones on attention, message and brand awareness, depth of processing, brand attitude and purchase intention. "New brands comparing themselves to established brands" gain most. https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-02017796.html [V] (abstract only; old but the strongest evidence found)
- In 2025-26 data: us-vs-them is 3.62% of ads, with a 6.52% hit rate and spend use 1.00 ("performs as expected"). Its best vertical is parenting (spend use 2.00, the top format there). Motion PDF [V]
- Motion's format library: it works when the win is "obvious, honest and demonstrable", mid-funnel, as split screens or side-by-side tests. Examples: Cozy Earth (comforter vs a generic one), BURGA (rival cases after six months), RYZE (vs a $10 Amazon frother), Kitsch ($20 vs expensive perfume). Motion warns that attacking named competitors can trigger Meta flags. https://runneth.com/library/formats/us-vs-them [V]
- Practitioners: imply the rival rather than naming it, and make "them" universal (the old way, a generic product); the format works best with warmer, consideration-stage viewers. https://www.bestever.ai/post/us-vs-them-ads [S]
- Meta's policy: the Advertising Standards have no comparative-ads section. What applies is "Ads may not contain content that violates the intellectual property rights of any third party", the deceptive / misleading practices rules, and "Ads must clearly represent the company, product ... being advertised." https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/ [V]
- US law: the FTC "encourages the naming of, or reference to competitors" and holds that "disparaging advertising is permissible so long as it is truthful and not deceptive". It requires clarity and, if needed, disclosure. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/14.15 [V]
- For Libra: the 2.97 ROAS "versus" video fits this evidence. Keep "them" generic (an open-top tote, a bag with outside pockets) and show each difference as a demo (zip shut, hidden back pocket). Never name a brand, and never aim the contrast at the viewer, because of Meta's personal-attributes rule (see the BFCM file). [I]

## 4. Testimonial and creator-story ads

- Partnership ads (Meta, 20 EMEA conversion-lift studies, Jan-Jun 2025, at least 30% of assets partnership ads): 45% lower median cost per incremental purchase and 35% higher median incremental ROAS, at 92% confidence. Meta's guidance:
  - Brief at least 30% of assets as partnership ads.
  - Run them inside the same ad sets as other ads, not standalone.
  - Ask creators for account-level permission, which lets the brand run their posts and make new partnership ads from them.
  https://ppc.land/meta-says-partnership-ads-cut-incremental-purchase-costs-45-in-emea-tests/ [V]
- The same creative run from the creator's handle instead of the brand's: +19% CTR, +10% conversion rate, -5% CPA (Agentio: 65,000 partnership ads, 137 brands, $130M). Martechvibe URL above [S]
- Creator testimonial in a partnership ad: +7.5% offsite conversion rate and +9.6% CTR (Meta's internal figure, reported by trade press). https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/meta-upgrades-partnership-ads-with-creator-testimonials-creator-content-in-collection-ads-and-a-new-partnership-ads-hub [U]
- What the best ones contain (Motion's testimonial library of 20 live ads):
  - A real customer to camera, telling problem → why she tried it → what changed.
  - Concrete details (a 45-minute class, the exact product name and size).
  - Casual first person, "not polished, not scripted to sound like an ad".
  Testimonials are 13.3% of all ads and 13.4% of spend (a scale format); confession hooks have spend use 1.41. https://runneth.com/library/formats/testimonial [V]
- Fit beats age: no single creator age, gender or follower band wins. Midlife creators win when they already talk about midlife topics; a stated age alone adds nothing. https://www.lightreel.ai/blogs/ugc-creator-demographics [S]
- How brands scale them (Caraway, Hers, Earnest on the Motion panel):
  - Creators come from the core persona, on retainers ("Right creators > more creators").
  - Briefs are fitted to each creator, and voiceover scripts are read aloud before sending.
  - A winning script is re-shot with a new persona (Hers: older women).
  Motion panel URL above [V]
- Counter-evidence: in apps, tutorials and reviews beat scripted testimonials (+45% installs per 1,000 impressions, AppsFlyer 2025), and polished ads out-earned creator-led ones in 13 of 17 accounts (Biddyco, above). https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/ugc-ads-apps [S]
- The law: the FTC rule (in force 21 Oct 2024) bans testimonials "by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews", and ones that "misrepresent the experience of the person giving it". https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials [V] The 2023 Endorsement Guides count virtual (AI) influencers as endorsers. https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2023/december/ftc-new-guidelines-for-endorsements-and-testimonials [S]
- Libra's numbers: 123-H4 = $29.8 CPA (ROAS about 2.8 at $84 AOV); 147-H1 = $28.4 CPA (ROAS about 3.0). These are the only ads at or above the 2.8 target, and both are real people's first-person stories, which an AI presenter may not legally imitate. [I]

## 5. AI-generated ads (avatars, AI UGC, AI statics)

- Columbia, Harvard, TUM and CMU on Taboola (Jan 2026; 500M+ impressions, matched "sibling" pairs from the same advertiser, campaign and day):
  - Raw CTR: AI 0.76% vs human 0.65%. In matched pairs the two are statistically equal.
  - AI ads that do not look AI beat both. Ads perceived as AI underperform, whoever made them.
  - Large, clear human faces were the strongest engagement signal.
  https://ppc.land/ai-ads-perform-as-well-as-humans-if-they-dont-look-ai-made/ [V]
- Marketing Science 2026 (Daviet and Nishimura), a live Instagram campaign: AI-generated background images reached 0.98% CTR vs 0.65% for a professional designer, and 3.38% vs 3.24% 18 months later. CTR only, one company. https://techxplore.com/news/2026-08-ai-generated-ads-outperform-human.html [V]
- A field experiment on Meta (JTAER 2026, 2x2 design, 384 observations): an AI-avatar spokesperson raised the video view rate (IRR 1.434) but cut CTR (IRR 0.707, p<0.001). Disclosing AI authorship had no significant effect. https://doi.org/10.3390/jtaer21100337 [U] (excerpts only; MDPI returned 403)
- NIQ (Dec 2024; 2,000+ viewers, about 150 measured by EEG): AI ads were called "annoying", "boring", "confusing" and produced weaker memory activation even when rated high quality. https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/ [S]
- Meta's own figures: campaigns using its image generation saw +11% CTR and +7.6% CVR; text generation +3% CTR. Demystifying URL above [V]
- AI UGC vs human UGC: no independent test exists. In one $15K skincare test, human UGC won on ROAS and AI won on variants per dollar; a human re-shoot of the winning AI hook beat the AI original. Reported weak spots for AI: emotional testimonials and hand-on-product demos. https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-ugc-vs-human-ugc-meta-ads-roas-2026 [S] One vendor claims AI conversion falls 8% above $100 order value. https://www.adbeacon.com/ai-creative-wins-clicks-loses-conversions/ [S]
- Labels: Meta now auto-detects third-party AI and shows "AI info" under "About this ad"; ads from Meta's own tools showing a photorealistic AI person get the label next to "Sponsored". Already covered in the BFCM file; https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/ [V]

## Top 10 takeaways for Libra

1. **Judge a batch by whether it finds a winner, not by its average.** Only 1 ad in 10-25 becomes a winner (Motion: 4-9% hit rate; ~55% of spend goes to winners). Give each new ad about $100-1,000 before judging: 45% of eventual winners still look like losers under $100 of spend. (§1)
2. **Count diversity per project, not per hook.** Meta groups ads with the same look and story as one creative, and agencies say the same opening hook counts as "essentially the same ad". So H1-H4 are iteration, and only the 5 projects (plus the statics) are separate bets. Each project needs its own persona, motivation, setting and format, ideally new talent or footage (Meta Dec 2025; Motion panel). (§2)
3. **Real customer stories are the proven route to 2.8+, so scale them as partnership ads.** Libra's two best ads, the evidence on partnership ads (-45% cost per incremental purchase; +19% CTR from the creator's handle) and Motion's testimonial anatomy (problem → try → change, concrete details) all point the same way. Brief at least 30% of assets as partnership ads, re-shoot winning scripts with new real women, and never have the AI presenter tell a first-person story (FTC). (§4)
4. **Make more "versus" ads.** The 2.97 ROAS comparison matches the meta-analysis (comparative ads lift attention and purchase intention, most for challenger brands). Use a generic "them", a visible demo of each difference, and no brand names. FTC allows truthful comparison; Meta has no ban, only its IP and misleading-claims rules. (§3)
5. **Text-forward statics are the volume engine.** Text-only ads win at 11.6% and product-image-with-text at 8.75%. Offer-first banners take 29% of all spend. In fashion, Post-it 27.4%, quiz 26.0%, stylized product shot 21.2%, social comments 14.8%; billboards and signs get extra spend. Add those formats to static_formats.json (true words only). (§1)
6. **Q4 hooks: concrete and news-like.** Newness, sale announcement, price anchor, urgency, offer only and confession hit 8.7-11.4% in the BFCM window, against 6.4-6.9% for authority, direct address and relatability. 55% of durable winners simply say what the product is. Put the real offer or the newest true fact into one of the four hooks, and drop vague lifestyle openers. (§1)
7. **The first second decides.** 82% of scaling ads show a text overlay in second one, and the product is seen working by second 3-8. Aim for 30%+ hook rate (40%+ is elite) and name the brand inside the first 3 s (+16% completion). (§1)
8. **Length: no evidence for or against 55-80 s.** Winners' median is 27-30 s; cost per result is flat across lengths (0.94-1.03x); hook rate is lowest over 60 s (16%). Rule 6 keeps our formats, so the honest move is to tell the owner this and let them decide on a controlled test of a 30-45 s body. Never trim a batch by ourselves. (§1)
9. **AI works when nobody can tell it is AI.** Matched studies show AI images equal or beat human ones on CTR unless they look AI. An AI-avatar spokesperson cut CTR by about 29% on Meta, and AI ads leave weaker memory traces. So keep AI to scenes, statics and gaps; keep real footage and real people for proof; treat the presenter project as a test, measured against the voiceover projects. (§5)
10. **Plan for Q4 fatigue, and lock what Meta may change.** Ecommerce creatives live a median of 22 days; winning creator ads about 36 days, then CPA rises 1.8x. Launch new concepts weekly through BFCM. Keep 9:16 with sound (+7% conversions per dollar), but turn off Advantage+ text rewriting and generated backgrounds, because they can alter claims or the bag. (§2)
